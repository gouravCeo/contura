How to Know If Your Bra Size Is Correct

By Sarah J. • May 13, 2026 • 4 min read

Most women wear the wrong bra size — estimates range from 60% to 80%. But how do you actually know if yours fits? Here are the five signs to check, and exactly what to do if something is off.

1. The Band Rides Up in the Back

Your band should sit parallel to the floor all the way around. If it's hiking up in the back, the band is too loose. Most of your support (about 80%) should come from the band, not the straps.

Fix it: Go down a band size. If you're wearing a 36, try a 34. Keep the same cup letter at first — but remember that a smaller band means a slightly smaller cup volume, so you may also need to go up a cup size (sister size). Check our sister size guide to find the right swap.

2. Straps Dig Into Your Shoulders

If your straps are leaving deep grooves, it means the band isn't doing its job. Straps should sit comfortably without sliding off or digging in. When the band is too loose, your shoulders compensate by carrying the full weight.

Fix it: Try a tighter band size first. If the band fits correctly and straps still dig, the cups may be too small — your breast tissue is pushing the cups outward, putting tension on the straps.

3. Spillage Over the Cups (Quad Boob)

If your breast tissue bulges over the top or sides of the cup, the cups are too small or the wrong shape. This is especially common in plunge and demi styles.

Fix it: Go up a cup size while keeping the same band. If you're overflowing a 34C, try 34D. If the band also feels tight, go up a band and down a cup (sister size: 36C).

4. Gaps or Wrinkles in the Cup

Visible sagging, wrinkling, or gaps at the top of the cup usually mean the cup is too big. But it can also mean the cup shape doesn't match your breast shape (e.g., you have shallow breasts but are wearing a full-coverage cup).

Fix it: Try a smaller cup size. If gaps persist, try a different style — balconette bras work well for shallow shapes, while full cups suit projected shapes.

5. The Center Gore Doesn't Lie Flat

The center piece between the cups (the gore) should rest flat against your sternum. If it's floating or tilting, the cups are too small or the band is too loose.

Fix it: The gore floating usually means go up a cup size. If the wires are sitting on breast tissue rather than your ribcage, the cups are too narrow or small.

A Quick Fit Checklist

Put on your bra and check these five things:

  1. Band is level and snug on the loosest hook
  2. Straps stay in place without digging
  3. No spillage over the cups or sides
  4. No gapping or wrinkling in the cups
  5. Center gore lies flat against your chest

When to Remeasure

Your bra size can change due to weight fluctuations, pregnancy, hormonal changes, and even aging. If your bras felt fine six months ago and now they don't, it's worth remeasuring. See our step-by-step measuring guide for the full process.

The Bottom Line

A correctly fitting bra should be comfortable enough to forget you're wearing it. If you're constantly adjusting straps, tugging at the band, or dealing with spillage, your size is wrong. Use the signs above to narrow down the issue, then make the adjustment.
